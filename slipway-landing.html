The problem

Nobody's first iOS build works. Yours fails for hours.

The code is the easy part. The launch is a maze of signing assets, store records, and config that live in four different places and fail with messages that don't tell you what to do. Sound familiar?

No matching profiles found for bundle identifier … "app_store" The profile exists in Apple's portal — but your CI reads from a different store entirely.
No valid code signing certificates were found Apple never hands back a private key, so the cert your build fetched can't actually sign anything.
Android: applicationId contains an invalid character '-' The bundle ID that's legal on iOS quietly breaks the Android build.
Auth works in dev, blank screen for testers An empty env var silently dropped the release build into a fake-login mode. No crash, no clue.
How it works

Four steps. One green build.

A guided path, in order — Slipway does the provisioning and hands the right config to your CI.

STEP 01

Connect your repo

Point Slipway at your Flutter, React Native, or native project and your Apple & Google accounts.

STEP 02

Answer the interview

A short guided Q&A: bundle ID, org accounts, backend endpoints. Slipway flags the traps before they cost a build.

STEP 03

It provisions everything

Certificates, profiles, store records, env config — created and synced to where the build actually looks.

STEP 04

Ship the first build

A signed build lands in TestFlight and Google Play internal testing. Invite testers and go.

What Slipway handles

The launch gauntlet, automated.

Every step here is a wall real teams hit on their first ship. Slipway clears them once and remembers.

Signing, solved once

Distribution cert and its private key created and stored together — then the profile is generated and synced where the build reads it, not just Apple's portal.

Store records, pre-flighted

App IDs, the numeric App Store ID, capabilities, the Play app record — created and wired before the first build, in the right order.

Config that can't silently break

Validates your production env vars and pings your endpoints, so a release never quietly falls back to a dev or fake-auth mode.

Bundle IDs that build on both

Catches the whole class of cross-platform mistakes — like a - that's legal on iOS but fails Android — before they cost you a run.

CI you keep

Generates your codemagic.yaml or Fastlane setup and sits on top of the CI you already run. No lock-in, no migration.

Every error mapped to a fix

When something does fail, you get the cause and the next action in plain language — not a stack trace and a forum search.
